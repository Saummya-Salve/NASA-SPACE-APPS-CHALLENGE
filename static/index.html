THE SCIENCE

About SPHEREx & our results

SPHEREx is a NASA infrared space telescope that surveys the sky in many wavelength bands. This project compares repeated observations to help users notice objects that move or change brightness.

Nearby stars and cold brown dwarfs are valuable targets. Infrared observations can reveal objects that are faint in visible light, while repeated views help measure their motion.

Key finding

Our scan finds Epsilon Indi B moving, yet it is not in Gaia at all: cold brown dwarfs are too faint in visible light for Gaia, but bright in SPHEREx's infrared. That is why SPHEREx can reveal nearby objects Gaia misses. Its spectrum shows water and methane absorption, the signature of a cold brown-dwarf atmosphere. WISE 0855, the coldest known brown dwarf, is invisible in single SPHEREx images, but stacking each six-month pass reveals it, and its measured motion matches the published value.

Limitations

Very bright (saturated) stars keep a small position offset. Faint objects measured on stacked images have larger uncertainties. Candidate movers need checking by eye. The sky map depends on the external CDS/Aladin service.

Data & tools

SPHEREx observations are fetched from NASA/IPAC IRSA and cached for 24 hours; the five challenge targets are pre-cached. The project uses Gaia DR3, Astropy, astroquery, reproject, Aladin Lite and Chart.js.
